Minnesota's Empty Lecterns

Four of Minnesota's eight House races have had debates. Both statewide races had empty lecterns on KSTP. And in the 6th District, two stations gave the time to the challenger after Rep. Tom Emmer didn't appear. Here's the full Minnesota record, both parties.

On the night of Oct. 2, KSTP set up lecterns for the three biggest statewide races in Minnesota. The station said it had sent invitations about six weeks earlier. The Republicans came: Lisa Demuth for governor, Michele Tafoya for Senate, Ron Schutz for attorney general. The DFL candidates, Amy Klobuchar, Peggy Flanagan and Keith Ellison, declined. Their lecterns stayed empty, and Tom Hauser spent the hour interviewing the Republicans.

A week earlier, KSTP had run an interview with Doug Chapin, the DFL candidate in the 6th Congressional District, and said it had reached out to Rep. Tom Emmer's campaign for one too. The day after the statewide debate night, KARE 11 aired Chapin on "On the Record." KARE said it invited both candidates. Emmer did not appear.

Those are four of the 30 events nationwide this fall where a host invited both candidates and gave the time to the one who came. Minnesota's are evenly split: two statewide events where DFL candidates stayed home, two in the 6th District where a Republican incumbent did.

This is the Minnesota piece of our series on the decline of debates. Parts 1 through 3 covered the national numbers, the reasons candidates give, and the formats that work.

The statewide races

Governor. Demuth accepted five debate invitations, including KSTP, FOX 9/Gray, TPT's "Almanac," the Duluth News Tribune and Duluth Chamber, and the Minnesota Chamber. Klobuchar accepted the Minnesota Chamber debate and one hosted by MPR News, which Demuth did not accept. The two eventually agreed on two: the Minnesota Chamber's debate in Brainerd on Oct. 19 and the FOX 9/Gray statewide televised debate in Rochester on Oct. 21.

Both came to Brainerd. The Oct. 19 forum was invite-only, moderated by KSTP's Tom Hauser and streamed by KSTP. MPR News called it "fairly tame with few direct exchanges." The two clashed most over state fraud. Demuth said she "will be firing all of the commissioners in the state of Minnesota and starting fresh." Klobuchar answered: "I'm different than Tim Walz. I know you wish you were debating Tim Walz. But I'm the one here right now." Both ruled out raising taxes. Both came to Rochester two nights later, too, for the only one of their meetings televised statewide. MPR News called it a "lively showdown," with each candidate tying the other to an unpopular figure. Klobuchar said "we need a governor who is not a rubber stamp of Donald Trump." Demuth said Klobuchar "never has once come up with a policy that she disagrees with Tim Walz on." They also split on abortion, the Boundary Waters mining order and gas prices. MPR noted the format limited follow-up questions, the same complaint Part 3 of this series took up.

Before those, they appeared back to back, not together, at the Economic Club of Minnesota on Oct. 5.

So each side turned down at least one host the other accepted: Klobuchar declined KSTP, and Demuth didn't accept MPR. KSTP's primetime hour went to Demuth alone. We found no record that MPR held a governor slot with Klobuchar alone.

Senate. Tafoya accepted KSTP; Flanagan accepted a FOX 9/KBJR debate in Duluth, which Tafoya hadn't committed to. The only debate both accepted was MPR's "Politics Friday" on Oct. 16, which MPR called their only scheduled meeting. Both showed up for the hour at MPR's St. Paul studios, moderated by Brian Bakst. They split hardest on immigration enforcement and tariffs. The Star Tribune reported this week that Tafoya's campaign didn't answer its request for a podcast interview, which Flanagan, Klobuchar and Demuth all sat for.

The House races

Four of Minnesota's eight districts have had general-election debates:

  • 1st District: Republican Rep. Brad Finstad and Democrat Jake Johnson debated twice, on KEYC/KTTC on Oct. 8 and KAAL on Oct. 13.
  • 2nd District (open seat): Democrat Matt Little and Republican Eric Pratt debated on KSTP on Oct. 2 and on MPR on Oct. 9.
  • 7th District: Republican Rep. Michelle Fischbach and Democrat Erik Osberg debated on Prairie Public on Oct. 15.
  • 8th District: Republican Rep. Pete Stauber and Democrat Trina Swanson debated on WDIO on Oct. 6.

Three Republican incumbents in Republican-leaning districts debated their challengers anyway.

In the other four, there has been no debate, or none we could find:

  • 3rd District: Democratic Rep. Kelly Morrison and Republican Tyler Bass. No record found.
  • 4th District: Democratic Rep. Betty McCollum and Republican Paul Wikstrom. No record found.
  • 5th District: Democratic Rep. Ilhan Omar, Republican John Nagel and Independent DeVelle L. Jackson. No debate.
  • 6th District: Republican Rep. Tom Emmer and Democrat Doug Chapin. No debate.

MN-06 Watch covers the 5th and 6th Districts closely enough to say it plainly: no general-election debate has been held or scheduled in either. For the 3rd and 4th, "no record found" means we checked news coverage, broadcaster listings, League of Women Voters forum pages and Vote411 and found nothing. It doesn't mean no invitation was made or declined. If a station or organization offered a debate in any of these districts, we want to hear about it and will add it to the tracker.

The 5th District shows why that matters. After Part 1 published, Jackson wrote to point out that our tracker had left him off, and he was right. We added him and logged the fix on our corrections page. He also told us he attended an Oct. 13 candidate forum hosted by the Minneapolis NAACP's political action committee, and that Omar and Nagel, who he says were both invited, did not. We haven't been able to confirm that forum independently yet, so the tracker lists it as claimed. Nagel told Alpha News, a conservative outlet, that his campaign had "reached out a couple of times" to Omar about debating and gotten no response. Jackson says he challenged both of them to debates in writing, and neither answered. We found no response from Omar's campaign on debates.

In the 3rd District, Republican Tyler Bass didn't answer candidate questionnaires from Eden Prairie Local News or the Hometown Source voters' guide. Rep. Kelly Morrison did. A questionnaire isn't a debate, but it's the same kind of no-answer we track in the 6th.

The 6th District

Emmer's seat is rated safe Republican. He is the House majority whip.

No general-election debate has been held or scheduled in the 6th District. Two stations invited him and gave the time to Chapin when he didn't appear: KSTP, which aired Chapin's interview in late September, and KARE 11, on Oct. 3. Neither station reported a stated reason from Emmer's campaign. MN-06 Watch has also confirmed that a local news outlet offered to host a debate. Chapin accepted; Emmer’s campaign did not respond.

Emmer has done interviews this fall. He sat for St. Cloud LIVE on Sept. 30 and the St. Cloud Times on Oct. 8, and KARE noted that he sat down with the station for an extensive interview in November 2025. He did not respond to candidate questionnaires from the Sun Patriot or the Monticello Times, or to one from MN-06 Watch. The Monticello Times wrote that Emmer "did not respond by the deadline, despite multiple requests," and the paper confirmed it to MN-06 Watch this week. He has not held an in-person town hall in more than 1,100 days.

Chapin has said publicly that he's ready to debate. He is actively campaigning for invitations and has set the same terms as Texas Senate candidate James Talarico: any time, any place, any network.

In a district rated safe, the general election may be the one point in the cycle where the likely winner gets asked hard questions next to an opponent. Part 2 of this series found that's where skipping is most predictable: of 36 candidates who declined in safe and likely seats nationally, 30 were favored to win.

The ask

Minnesota's stations have done better than most. KSTP held its debate night without the DFL candidates and interviewed the Republicans who came. KARE gave Chapin the airtime it offered both. Four districts got real debates, and the governor's race got two.

There are 12 days until the election.

On Oct. 19, MN-06 Watch sent the same request to every newsroom covering these districts that we could reach: KSTP, KARE 11, WCCO, FOX 9, MPR News, Minnesota PBS, the Star Tribune, the St. Cloud Times, St. Cloud LIVE, KNSI and the local papers across the 6th District. Put a 6th District forum on the calendar. Invite both candidates in writing, publish the invitation, and hold the hour whoever comes. Do the same in the 3rd, 4th and 5th if no one has. As of Oct. 21, the replies had come from one newspaper group, Adams MultiMedia, which publishes the Monticello Times, the Elk River Star News and other papers in the district. The Monticello Times confirmed Emmer hadn't answered its questionnaire. The group's news director said its papers were finishing voter guides this week, and that those guides note which candidates didn't respond. Neither offered a forum.

If you won’t sit for the job interview, why should the constituents hire you?
